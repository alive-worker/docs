Too Many AI Tools: Why We Forgot How to Choose

Two years ago, finding a usable AI tool took some digging. Now open any leaderboard and a single task has dozens of products fighting to do it for you. Tools went from scarce to overflowing, yet oddly, more choice has left many people more hesitant, more anxious, and sometimes using nothing at all. This piece isn't about whether product X beats product Y. It steps back to look at choice overload itself: why more tools make picking harder, what an AI tool comparison should actually measure, whether we're outsourcing our own judgment, and once you finally settle on a few tools, how to pull those scattered subscription bills into one place.

ipfsWeb · AI Access Guide Editorial · Published 2026-08-16 14:23:25

1. From "can't find a tool" to "can't pick one" in two years

Think back to the first time you used AI to write, draw, or fix code. Back then the usable products were few, and landing on one that just worked felt like luck. Now it's flipped: pick any narrow task — writing, voiceover, video, meeting notes — and a long line of products stands behind it, features heavily overlapping, taglines nearly identical. Tools went from scarce resource to oversupply, which should be a win, but it quietly changed our relationship with them. The old question was "does one exist?" The new one is "which do I pick?" — and the second is far more tiring.

2. More choice, but no more satisfaction

There's a classic finding in psychology: when the options grow from three to thirty, deciding gets harder and regret afterward gets stronger — you keep suspecting the road not taken was better. With AI tools it's textbook. You subscribe to a writing assistant, it's fine, but every week someone pushes a new one and you can't help wondering "should I switch?" So your time goes not into using tools but into comparing them. The sneakiest cost of choice overload isn't picking wrong — it's getting stuck in a permanent state of picking, never settling down long enough to finish anything.

3. Most AI tool comparisons measure the wrong thing

Open any AI tool comparison and you'll mostly find a spec table: whose context window is longer, who scores higher, who supports one more language. Useful, sure, but it assumes tools exist to be ranked against each other. In reality you're not crowning an all-around champion — you're picking a partner that slots into your day. A tool that ranks second but fits your existing habits perfectly is usually worth keeping over the chart-topper that demands you relearn everything. A good AI tool comparison doesn't ask who is strongest; it asks who fits your own AI use cases best.

4. Are we outsourcing our own judgment?

There's a subtler thing worth watching. When tools pile up beyond what anyone can review, a lot of people cope by not judging at all — they just check who ranks first, whichever the loudest video pushes, and buy that. Convenient short-term, but over time it dulls our own judgment: you increasingly don't know what you actually need, only that "everyone's using this one." Tools are supposed to extend our abilities, yet once we hand off the act of choosing too, we drift from being people who use tools to people led around by tool leaderboards. Keeping a bit of patience to test and compare things yourself is really about holding on to your own agency.

5. Subtract: pick "good enough" instead of chasing "the best"

The way out of choice overload isn't finding the single best — it's decisively locking in a few "good enough" tools and then stopping. A practical move: list the scenarios you genuinely hit often — probably no more than three or four, like daily writing, coding help, the occasional image — and keep exactly one main tool per scenario, setting the rest aside. Give yourself a rule, say no switching for three months just because "I heard there's a better one." You'll find that spending the reclaimed comparison time on actually mastering your tools yields more. Choosing good enough and then focusing almost always beats endlessly chasing optimal.

6. Once the tools are chosen, the billing gets messy

Even if you disciplined yourself down to three or four regulars, another headache surfaces: most are overseas subscriptions, each charging separately, each with its own renewal date and exchange-rate markup. Reconcile at month's end and you realize the money went out in a fog. You subtracted your choices, but payment stayed scattered. 8. Wrapping up

As tools go from scarce to overflowing, the test is no longer our ability to find one but our composure not to drown in choices. Don't turn AI tool comparison into a spec-sheet arms race — compare fit to your scenarios instead; don't outsource judgment to leaderboards — keep some patience to test yourself; pick good enough and then focus, rather than chasing optimal forever. Once your regulars converge to three or four, use one virtual card to pull the subscription bills together, and both choice and payment turn calm.
